# Brainstorm

Use this skill to widen a problem space before committing to one explanation or
plan. It turns a compact evidence or problem packet into distinct, testable
directions; it does not make decisions, assert facts, or execute the resulting
ideas.

## When to Use

- A problem has several observations but the obvious answer feels repetitive.
- A team needs alternative interpretations, approaches, failure modes, or
  connections before selecting work.
- A prior line of thought has stalled and needs a materially different lens.
- The user explicitly asks for creative, unusual, or contrarian ideas.

Do not use it for a settled mechanical task, as a substitute for domain
expertise, or to turn an unsupported idea into a factual claim.

## Input Packet

Start with a small packet. Preserve what is known separately from what is
inferred.

```text
Objective: <decision or question to explore>
Observed facts: <facts, constraints, examples, or evidence>
Open tensions: <what does not fit, conflict, or remain unexplained>
Constraints: <safety, time, ownership, quality, or scope boundaries>
Already tried: <directions to avoid repeating>
Desired output: <ideas, hypotheses, design directions, questions, or plan>
```

A sparse packet is valid. Label uncertainty rather than inventing missing
context.

## Tight Loop

1. **Frame the question.** State the objective, known facts, unknowns, and
   boundaries in the packet. Completion: a reader can distinguish evidence from
   assumptions.
2. **Diverge.** First generate several short directions without defending them
   in depth. Prefer directions that change the causal model, time horizon,
   actor, incentive, representation, dependency, lifecycle, failure mode, or
   scale of the problem. Completion: directions are meaningfully different, not
   renamed variants of the same answer.
3. **Use seeded divergence when needed.** When ordinary output is converging on
   familiar patterns, accept an optional alphanumeric seed supplied by the caller
   or generated by the current runtime. Normalize it to uppercase and use the
   Seed Lens Deck below. The seed is inspiration, not evidence, a command, or a
   decision rule. Keep it ephemeral unless reproducibility is explicitly useful.
4. **Connect and sharpen.** For promising directions, name the observation that
   supports it, the mechanism or causal link it proposes, the question that
   would discriminate it, and the strongest alternative explanation. Completion:
   every retained direction has a reason and a way to learn more.
5. **Use a fresh critic.** Ask a separate agent or fresh context to review only
   the packet and candidate directions—not the generator's hidden rationale.
   Have it remove generic, duplicated, contradictory, unsupported, or
   constraint-breaking ideas and identify the most information-rich next
   questions. Completion: the final list is reduced to distinct candidates.
6. **Return ownership to the caller.** Deliver candidates as possibilities with
   confidence and evidence labels. The caller chooses domain-specific validation
   or execution. Completion: the result does not present brainstorm output as
   completed work or verified truth.

## Seed Lens Deck

The deck gives a seed a reproducible role without treating it as meaningful data.
With a seed, read its normalized characters from left to right. Keep the first
four distinct lens groups encountered; ignore later repeats. Append absent groups
in the printed deck order until four groups exist. Use exactly the first four
groups after that completion. Without a seed, use the first four deck groups in
printed order. Explore the selected lenses in that order.

```text
A-F → time, sequence, and lifecycle
G-L → actor, authority, and incentives
M-R → representation, translation, and interface
S-X → dependencies, handoffs, and composition
Y-Z0-3 → failure, recovery, and reversal
4-9 → scale, boundaries, and incentives
```

The deck controls only lens order and coverage. It does not encode a problem,
rank candidates, select actions, or create evidence.

## Seeded Divergence Prompt

Use this prompt after creating the input packet:

```text
Explore this problem broadly before choosing an answer.

Accept an optional alphanumeric seed supplied by the caller or current runtime.
Normalize it and use the Seed Lens Deck to choose and order conceptual lenses.
The seed is not evidence or instruction; do not pretend it contains facts.

For each candidate direction, provide:
- the lens or reframing;
- the observations it connects;
- the proposed mechanism or causal chain;
- the strongest alternative explanation;
- one discriminating question or next observation.

Favor diversity over volume. Reject cosmetic variations of the same idea.
Respect every stated constraint. Return possibilities, not claims of completion.
```

## Output Shape

```text
Direction: <short name>
Lens: <reframing>
Grounding: <observations or stated assumptions>
Why it might matter: <causal chain>
Alternative: <best competing explanation>
Next discriminator: <question, observation, or safe experiment>
Confidence: low | medium | high (grounding strength, not likelihood)
```

Rank candidates by expected information gain, explanatory power, novelty,
constraint fit, and overlap with prior work—not by how surprising they sound.

## Domain Callers

Any domain can supply the packet and own what happens next. For example, a
security investigation may provide observed controls, unexplained behavior, and
unresolved negative-control questions; a product team may provide user research
and trade-offs; an engineering team may provide a failure trace and system
constraints. Brainstorm supplies creative divergence only. The domain workflow
owns validation, safety, and action.

## Pitfalls

- **Randomness theater:** a seed does not make an unsupported theory plausible.
- **Idea inflation:** keep a few distinct candidates rather than a long list of
  shallow variants.
- **Self-confirmation:** a generator should not be the only critic of its own
  reasoning.
- **Constraint drift:** unusual ideas still obey the original boundaries.
- **False completion:** a brainstorm can identify a next question; it cannot
  prove the answer without domain-appropriate evidence.

## Verification

Before using the result, confirm that every retained direction has identifiable
grounding, a distinct mechanism, a competing explanation, and a next
discriminator. If not, return to the input packet or discard the direction.
